About the Collatz Conjecture

The Collatz Conjecture (also known as the \(3n + 1\) problem, Hailstone sequence, or Syracuse problem) is one of mathematics' most infamous open problems, proposed by Lothar Collatz in 1937.

The Rules:

Take any positive integer n:

  • If n is even, divide it by 2: f(n) = n / 2
  • If n is odd, multiply by 3 and add 1: f(n) = 3n + 1

The Conjecture:

No matter what starting positive integer n you choose, the sequence will always eventually reach 1 (and enter the repeating cycle 4 → 2 → 1).

Why "Hailstone"?

The numbers bounce up and down dramatically—like hailstones inside a thunderstorm cloud—before eventually descending to Earth (the number 1).
